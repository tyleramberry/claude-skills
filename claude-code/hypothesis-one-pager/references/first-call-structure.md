# First Call Structure (for reading transcripts)

Space Tiger's first call runs ~30 minutes and follows this outline. Knowing the
structure helps you locate the discovery answers inside a raw transcript, where
speaker labels and question phrasing will vary.

1. **Open & Agenda** — rapport, confirm time, set agenda.
2. **Background on the Client** — client talks about their business/team, open-ended.
3. **Space Tiger's Background** — brief, kept short.
4. **Discovery** — the 8 questions that matter for the Hypothesis Doc:
   1. Walk me through what's happening today? *(current state / process)*
   2. What's the specific problem or moment that pushed you to look into this now? *(trigger)*
   3. What have you already tried — tools, other agencies, an in-house attempt — and what happened? *(prior attempts)*
   4. What systems or tools does your team rely on daily that this would need to work alongside? *(existing stack)*
   5. Who else is involved in this decision, and who'd own it day-to-day once it's live? *(stakeholders/owner)*
   6. Is there a timeline you're working against — a deadline, renewal, or event driving urgency? *(timeline)*
   7. What budget range have you set aside for this, or what feels right given the outcome you're after? *(budget — internal use only, never in client-facing output)*
   8. Other
5. **Wrap-Up & Next Step** — reflect back what was heard, introduce the Blueprint call as next step.

## Reading the transcript

- Answers won't arrive in this exact order — people circle back and volunteer things
  early. Read the whole transcript before drafting; don't assume Q1's answer is the
  first thing said.
- Question 1 (current state) and Question 4 (existing stack) are the primary source
  for Pain Points and for judging Enablement vs. Automation vs. Custom.
- Question 6 (timeline) feeds the "note the deadline" rule in the Hypothesis Doc.
- Question 7 (budget) must never leak into the client-facing doc — no dollar figures
  anywhere in Pain Points or Hypotheses. It's fine to let it inform which tier feels
  realistic in your own reasoning, silently.
